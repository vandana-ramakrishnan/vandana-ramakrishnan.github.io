Working Papers

Tariff Attention and Inflation Expectations: Evidence from Financial Markets

With Farah Tohme

Tariff-related news drew contrasting inflation-compensation responses across the two U.S. trade-war episodes: TIPS breakevens and inflation swaps declined in 2018–2021, whereas in 2025 inflation swaps rose while short-maturity breakevens fell. The contrast underscores the importance of instrument-specific factors when interpreting market inflation signals.

Inflation Expectations Anchoring and the State-Dependent Phillips Curve

Inflation responds more strongly to macroeconomic shocks when inflation expectations are less anchored. My estimates suggest a steeper Phillips curve in low-anchoring states, highlighting why expectations anchoring matters for inflation dynamics and monetary policy.

Tariff News and Household Inflation Expectations: Role of Prior Beliefs

Household inflation expectations respond more around especially salient tariff announcements. The responses are larger among less financially sophisticated households and those with less anchored prior beliefs, suggesting that trade-policy news transmits unevenly across households.

This project examines how banks’ transaction fees and participation in the Federal Reserve’s par collection system may have affected regional deposit patterns and financial integration in the United States.
