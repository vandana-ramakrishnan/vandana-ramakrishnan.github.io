Inflation Expectations Anchoring and the State-Dependent Phillips Curve

Abstract

Does the slope of the Phillips curve depend on how firmly inflation expectations are anchored? I examine this question using an interacted vector autoregression in which inflation dynamics vary with survey-based measures of expectations anchoring. Drawing on microdata from the Survey of Professional Forecasters and the University of Michigan, I estimate the model for the United States over 1968Q4–2026Q1. I find that the cumulative inflation response to an identified demand shock is approximately three times larger when expectations are unanchored than when they are anchored. The difference reflects stronger shock transmission rather than larger demand shocks in the unanchored regime. Regional U.S. evidence also shows a steeper inflation–unemployment relationship where household expectations are less anchored, while estimates for Brazil and the euro area support the broader pattern. A historical counterfactual suggests that the same shocks experienced during 2021–23 would have raised peak core inflation to approximately 10%, rather than the observed 6.4%, under 1970s-level anchoring. The findings highlight the role of expectations anchoring in shaping inflation dynamics and the transmission of macroeconomic shocks.
