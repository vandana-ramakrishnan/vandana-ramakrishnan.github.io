Tariff News and Household Inflation Expectations: Role of Prior Beliefs

Abstract

Do major tariff announcements change household inflation expectations, and which households respond? I examine seven U.S. tariff episodes between 2018 and 2025, using Google Trends to identify peaks in public search attention and daily interview dates from the New York Fed’s Survey of Consumer Expectations (SCE) to compare within-person inflation-forecast revisions around each peak. One-year expectations rise significantly at the baseline window around the 2018 steel and aluminum tariffs (1.18 percentage points) and the July 2025 reinstatement of reciprocal tariffs (1.72 points). A separate Michigan panel records a large April 2025 revision around Liberation Day. In the two positive SCE episodes, revisions are larger among households whose previous expectations were unanchored. Low financial literacy predicts a larger response in several episodes, while measured consumption, housing and geographic tariff exposure do not systematically predict revisions. These patterns suggest an important role for prior beliefs in the transmission of policy news to expectations.
