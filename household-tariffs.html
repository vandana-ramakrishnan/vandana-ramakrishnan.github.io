Tariff News and Household Inflation Expectations: Role of Prior Beliefs

Overview

I analyze how public attention to tariff announcements shapes household inflation expectations and how responses differ across households. Using Google Trends and individual-level data from the Survey of Consumer Expectations and University of Michigan surveys, I exploit variation in survey timing around tariff-attention peaks across seven U.S. tariff episodes. I find that expectation revisions are concentrated around highly salient announcements, with stronger responses among financially less sophisticated households and those with less anchored prior expectations. The findings highlight the role of attention and expectations anchoring in the transmission of trade policy news to household inflation expectations.
